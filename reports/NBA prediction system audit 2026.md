# Teach the Model What the Market Knows

Cross-referencing betting odds makes the forecasts better only by replacing the model: on 3,224 identical walk-forward games (November 2023 to April 2026) the de-vigged closing line scores **70.2% accuracy and a Brier score of 0.194**, the shipped blend **68.9% and 0.204**, and a fitted stack of the two lands on the market's own numbers (**70.1%, 0.194**) with the model's weight averaging **−0.29** ([market_lab.json](../output/market_lab.json)). That result puts the system's weakness in its information rather than its learner, whose accuracy on its own training rows (0.684) equals its out-of-sample score. The audit finds the gaps the market exploits: more than a quarter of the model's gain rests on a roster block read from the post-game box score, there is no injury-report feed and no handling of rest, travel or rookies, the evaluation never scores a team's first ten games, which is where the season stands today, and the pipeline cannot forecast any 2026-27 game because its caches end with 2025-26 and depend on an offline phone database. The 2025-26 state of the art points the same way, toward team strength built from each player's impact rating times his projected minutes (EPM, DARKO, FiveThirtyEight's RAPTOR-Elo blend), fed by an official injury report that now refreshes every 15 minutes, can be archived back to 2018-19, and must be read as probabilities: a "Questionable" player played 48% of the time in 2026. The order of improvement follows from that diagnosis: the as-of pipeline and injury archive first, then an evaluation able to detect small gains, then a team-strength input built from availability, ratings and minutes, then calibration. Odds are worth keeping in four roles — the gate every change must pass, a preseason prior for a season simulator that today does no better than "same as last season", a map of where the model misreads games, and the pending opening-line test — but not as tree features or as a shipped "model + market" forecast. That opening-line test will also flatter the model unless availability is taken from the injury report as it stood when the line opened.

## Honest skill trails the closing line on every metric

The project is more rigorous than most hobby systems. It found and measured two real leaks, roster features read from the finished game and a test set that chose tree counts, it keeps its negative results, and it has always treated the market as an outside yardstick rather than a target ([DURUM.md L36-63](../DURUM.md), the project's status file). Its binding scoreboard is an expanding-window walk-forward over 18 test months from November 2023 to April 2026, three seeds each, that scores only games in which both teams have played at least ten times. On it the production classifier picks **66.6%** of winners and the shipped blend, an equal-weight average of the classifier, a margin model mapped to a probability and a logistic model, picks **68.7%**, with Brier scores of 0.211 and 0.204 and a margin error of 11.2 points ([DURUM.md L14-28](../DURUM.md); [metrics_2025_26.json](../output/metrics_2025_26.json)). Several figures below are re-measurements made during this audit with read-only scripts run on the saved models and outputs (audit checks A to C). The table places the system among public benchmarks; only the two rows marked "same games" are directly comparable.

The system sits far above ratings built only from results and level with the best public player model on picking winners, which is where accuracy saturates. The remaining room lies in probability quality and margin error, and there the same-game comparison is clear. Paired within cells, with standard errors clustered by month, the blend trails the line by **1.3 points of accuracy (SE 0.6), 0.010 Brier (SE 0.002) and 0.026 log loss (SE 0.005)**, the margin model trails the closing spread by **0.51 points of MAE (SE 0.10)**, and the blend's Brier is worse than the market's in 48 of 54 cells (my calculation from [market_lab.json](../output/market_lab.json)). The earlier 308-game check that had the two tied on picking winners (0.773 against 0.776) was a small-sample result ([market_check.json](../output/market_check.json)). A 0.010 Brier gap is large next to what published method changes buy, since MOVDA's gain over TrueSkill is about 0.004 ([arXiv 2506.00348](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00348v1)), and even a commercial modeller concedes that its NBA models have not reached the closing line's Brier ([PropsBot](https://propsbot.ai/brier-score/)).

Two of the project's own headline figures overstate this skill. The **0.773 "held-out" accuracy** comes from the last 308 games of 2025-26, the season's easiest stretch: their home-win rate was 0.614 against about 0.555 for the season, the walk-forward's own March and April 2026 months scored 0.766 and 0.809 against 0.589 in December, the closing line hit 0.776 on the same games, and a bootstrap puts the 95% interval at 0.727-0.818 ([metrics_2025_26.json](../output/metrics_2025_26.json); [impact_v5_lab.json](../output/impact_v5_lab.json); [audit check A](../research_notes/NBA%20prediction%20system%20audit%202026/checks/prod_check.py)). Those games also sit inside the folds on which the features, recipe and impact pricing were chosen, so they were never fitted on but were used in model selection ([DURUM.md L69-95](../DURUM.md)). The **0.687 walk-forward figure** is honest for mid-November to April but says nothing about October, because the ten-game burn-in drops 1,400 of 10,749 games (13%), every opening month among them ([walk_forward.py L33-37](../prediction_engines/walk_forward.py)). An older walk-forward that kept those games, run on an earlier 190-feature dataset, scored **0.611, 0.541 and 0.635** in October 2023, 2024 and 2025 ([walk_forward_2025_26.json](../output/walk_forward_2025_26.json)), while the closing line scored 0.685, 0.620 and 0.638 in the same months (my calculation from [odds_history.csv](../odds_data/odds_history.csv)). The market weakens early too, scoring 67.2% and Brier 0.205 in the 466 games of 2023-26 that the burn-in excludes against 69.7% and 0.196 in the scored ones, but the model weakens more because its team features roll straight through the summer: a team's ten-game margin average correlates 0.38 with its final season value at the opener, against 0.75 from game ten on ([engineered_dataset_pregame.pkl](../output/engineered_dataset_pregame.pkl)). On 26 October 2026, every game falls in the regime the headline number excludes.

The statistics behind the lab's choices are looser than they look. Standard errors divide by √54 even though each month's three seeds share the same test games, so clustering by month widens them, and about 30 comparisons of model variants ran on the same folds; together these put the shipped recipe's accuracy gain (t ≈ 2.15) within what selection alone can produce, while its Brier gain (t ≈ 3.8) survives ([consistency_lab_round2.json](../output/consistency_lab_round2.json); [audit check C](../research_notes/NBA%20prediction%20system%20audit%202026/checks/labs.py)). Candidate feature families were screened on accuracy, the least sensitive metric, so adding them all moved Brier by −0.0030 without the screen registering it ([walk_forward_7arms.json](../output/walk_forward_7arms.json)). The lab also scores a sibling of the shipped artifact, one LightGBM per cell trained on 80% of prior games, rather than the ten-seed refit that actually ships, whose score models were never evaluated ([consistency_lab.py L172-204](../prediction_engines/consistency_lab.py); [retrain_production.py L91-98](../prediction_engines/retrain_production.py)). The literature prescribes exactly the missing pieces: proper scoring rules and reliability analysis instead of accuracy or betting returns ([Walsh & Joshi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06021); [Wunderlich & Memmert](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v36y2020i2p713-722.html)), correction for every variant tried ([JockeyBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19589)), and a test set fixed in advance, as the APBR projection contest requires ([APBR 2024-25](https://apbr.org/metrics/viewtopic.php?t=10146&start=240)). For this project the natural pre-registered test is 2026-27 itself, forecast before each tip-off with a frozen recipe and scored against the market afterwards.

### The dashboard repeats the flattering numbers

The dashboard is candid by hobby standards, with in-sample and held-out badges on every game, a note on how the numbers were measured and a SIM tag with a trust warning, yet its figures mislead in small ways that add up. The header pairs the blend's 0.687 with the classifier's AUC and with "±0.066", which is the classifier's month-to-month spread rather than an uncertainty for the blend's mean (the 95% interval from the 18 monthly results is about 0.661-0.712); it shows no Brier score and no market benchmark, and it carries stale constants such as "21 months" and "12 months, 0.667" ([app.py L78-117](../app.py); [index.html L220-224](../templates/index.html)). It tells users the models never saw the 308 held-out games and badges them "true out-of-sample", ignoring their use in model selection ([index.html L227-228](../templates/index.html); [app.py L123-126](../app.py)). The team header's model accuracy pools mostly training games, and the "empty stats" badge and the game-detail roster panel still rank players by the discredited v4 impact score, which priced every rebound as offensive and charged nothing for misses ([power_rankings.py L300-315](../prediction_engines/power_rankings.py); [app.py L144-182](../app.py)). In the rankings, season strength jumps from an Elo conversion to a lightly regularised Massey solve once 100 league games exist, the Elo home edge of 100 points (about 3.6 points of margin) is twice the 1.7-point home margin measured in 2025-26, and a weekly player rating can rest on a single game ([power_rankings.py L74-118, L238-248](../prediction_engines/power_rankings.py); [DURUM.md L193-195](../DURUM.md)). The only 2026-27 content is the preseason simulation shown as one random draw, which picked the real winner in 15 of 28 games ([sim_vs_real_2026_2027.json](../output/sim_vs_real_2026_2027.json)). Each fix is small: report one arm's accuracy, Brier and log loss with intervals, add the market's figures on the same games, relabel the 308 games "not fitted on; used in model selection", show an early-season warning through November, base the badges on the value score, and order simulated standings by the mean over replications.

## The model is short of information, not capacity

Overfitting is not the binding constraint. With 15-leaf trees, at least 60 games per leaf and early stopping, the shipped blend scores 0.684 on its own training rows, the same as out of sample, and its 196 heavily redundant columns behave like 30-50 effective dimensions, with 32 principal components explaining 90% of their variance ([audit check A](../research_notes/NBA%20prediction%20system%20audit%202026/checks/prod_check.py); [audit check B](../research_notes/NBA%20prediction%20system%20audit%202026/checks/prod_check2.py)). Five candidate feature families, namely opponent ratings, rest, clutch, availability and season phase, produced no measurable accuracy gain ([DURUM.md L69-95](../DURUM.md)). The limits lie in how the model turns signal into probabilities and in what signal it has.

### Probabilities are too timid

On the 308 held-out games a logistic recalibration of the blend has a slope of **1.58**, and the quintile forecast at 0.70 won 91.8% of the time. The market's own slope on those games was 1.31, so the easy window explains part of this, but the spread of the forecasts tells the same story everywhere: the blend's win probabilities have a standard deviation of 0.213 against the market's 0.26, its predicted margins 7.9 points against the closing spread's 10.2, and its predicted totals 4.7 against the closing total's 8.2 ([audit check A](../research_notes/NBA%20prediction%20system%20audit%202026/checks/prod_check.py); [audit check B](../research_notes/NBA%20prediction%20system%20audit%202026/checks/prod_check2.py)). Three design choices compound the timidity. The blend's one-third weights were justified by an experiment run before the roster leak was fixed and have never been fitted ([ensemble_model.py L77-131](../prediction_engines/ensemble_model.py)). The calibrator is chosen on the slice it was fitted on, where isotonic regression cannot lose, and the shipped blend does not apply it anyway ([retrain_production.py L352-358](../prediction_engines/retrain_production.py)). And missing values become zeros, so "scored zero points in the last ten games" is a value that both the trees and the logistic model see ([build_dataset_db.py L211](../prediction_engines/build_dataset_db.py)). The remedies are standard: archive each component's per-game walk-forward probabilities, fit the blend weights by logistic stacking inside a nested walk-forward, recalibrate the combined output with Platt or beta scaling, and leave gaps as NaN for LightGBM to handle natively. The dashboard's score intervals come from yet another, weaker model, a single-seed Poisson LightGBM that scores Brier 0.185 and margin MAE 12.0 on the held-out games against 0.167 and 11.5 for the shipped models, and they should be centred on the shipped predictions instead ([simulation_2025_26.json](../output/simulation_2025_26.json)).

### More than a quarter of the signal is hindsight about who played

The 24 roster and matchup features are 12% of the columns but carry **30.3% of the winner model's split gain and 26.3% of the margin model's** (re-measured on the shipped ten-member ensembles in [models/](../models/)). They are computed from the post-game box score of the very game being predicted. The "dressed" roster is everyone listed minus anyone tagged unavailable, and "DNP – Injury/Illness" counts as unavailable even though the player was on the active list; there are 1,957 such rows since 2017-18, about two-thirds of them belonging to rotation players averaging 20 or more minutes ([pregame_roster.py L120-133, L288-302](../prediction_engines/pregame_roster.py)). This is not a leak of the result, which the project fixed in October, but hindsight about availability, in which every Questionable, Doubtful and game-time decision arrives already resolved. A real forecaster sees the official injury report instead, which teams must file by 5 p.m. local time the day before a game and refile between 11 a.m. and 1 p.m. on game day, and which the league has republished every 15 minutes since late December 2025 ([NBA Official](https://official.nba.com/nba-injury-report-2026-27-season/); [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/47361909/nba-outlines-injury-info-prop-betting-changes-memo-teams)). Its statuses are probabilities, and badly labelled ones. Across 2,007 first listings from January to October 2026, **Questionable players played 48.3%** of the time, Probable players 86.6% and Doubtful players 4.2%, against team-published keys of 50, 75 and 25%, and the Questionable rate ranged from about 22% to 68% by team, on samples as small as 23 tags ([IsHePlayingTonight](https://isheplayingtonight.com/nba-injuries/questionable-play-rate); [Kings injury key](https://www.nba.com/kings/blog/injury-kings-clippers)). The walk-forward therefore describes a forecaster who already knows the final active list. How far that overstates live skill cannot be measured until injury reports are archived, because a box score cannot say which DNP-Injury players had been listed Out beforehand.

### Rest, travel, opponents and newcomers are missing

Most of what published systems treat as the next-largest inputs is absent. Rest and back-to-back flags are computed and stored but are not among the 196 features, although the raw split is wide: with neither team on a back-to-back the home side wins by 1.85 points and 55.6% of the time, with only the home team on one by −0.49 and 49.2%, and with only the visitor on one by 4.04 and 61.1% (my calculation from [engineered_dataset_pregame.pkl](../output/engineered_dataset_pregame.pkl), not adjusted for team strength). Published research gives effects of the same order. FiveThirtyEight charged 46 Elo points, about 1.6 points of margin, for a back-to-back, penalised travel distance and gave altitude a bonus ([538 methodology](https://web.archive.org/web/20240101081135/https://fivethirtyeight.com/methodology/how-our-nba-predictions-work/)); eastward jet lag cost home teams 1.29 points ([Frontiers in Physiology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2022.892681)); and teams travelling home for the second game of a back-to-back lost about 4% in win likelihood per extra 500 km ([J Clin Sleep Med](https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.9446)). The schedule file already holds tip-off times and arena cities that no feature uses ([fetch_schedule.py L40-64](../data_retriaval/fetch_schedule.py)). The box-score features are raw per-game counts with no pace or opponent adjustment and no free throws, even though the master frame already loads possessions, offensive and defensive ratings and the four factors ([csv_master.py L58-65](../prediction_engines/csv_master.py)), and the Elo and Massey ratings in [team_ratings.py](../prediction_engines/team_ratings.py) go unused. Opponent ratings and rest were both rejected on accuracy, and both verdicts are suspect. The ratings arm's margin MAE of 10.47, far below the honest 11.2, suggests it ran on the old leaky dataset ([walk_forward_7arms.json](../output/walk_forward_7arms.json)), and the hindsight roster block soaks up part of any rest effect, because a star resting on a back-to-back appears in it as absent. Both deserve a re-test on paired Brier with honest features. Home advantage should also vary over time, since home teams won about 60% of games in 2000-13 but 54-56% in most seasons since 2020-21 ([Yahoo Sports](https://sports.yahoo.com/fact-or-fiction-is-home-court-advantage-in-the-nbas-regular-season-dead-194958543.html)).

New players make every one of these gaps worse. A debuting rookie has no history, so the roster metrics skip him and the top-eight sum promotes a bench veteran in his place; after one game his value is a one-game average with no prior; and a player back from a long absence carries his pre-injury values at full minutes ([pregame_roster.py L180-237](../prediction_engines/pregame_roster.py)). Season averages carry last season's full value at a team's first game and jump to a one-game sample at its second, so over games one to three their correlation with the final value falls to 0.34 ([engineered_dataset_pregame.pkl](../output/engineered_dataset_pregame.pkl)). Published systems handle this with explicit priors: FiveThirtyEight phased a player's in-season rating in between 100 and 1,000 minutes and reverted team Elo a quarter of the way to the mean each summer ([538 methodology](https://web.archive.org/web/20240101081135/https://fivethirtyeight.com/methodology/how-our-nba-predictions-work/); [538 Elo](https://web.archive.org/web/20240105144602/https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/how-we-calculate-nba-elo-ratings/)), and EPM's baseline starts from a preseason projection that fades ([Dunks & Threes](https://dunksandthrees.com/about/epm)). The gap is expensive this season, after a summer that moved Giannis Antetokounmpo to Miami, Jaylen Brown to Philadelphia, LaMelo Ball to Minnesota, Ja Morant to Portland and Julius Randle to Brooklyn ([NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/power-rankings-2026-27-week-1)), and with the rookies Cameron Boozer and Darius Acuff Jr. each playing about 70 minutes in their first two games ([sim_vs_real_2026_2027.json](../output/sim_vs_real_2026_2027.json)).

### Player ratings times projected minutes is the published answer

The strongest public systems solve these problems with one structure: each player expected to dress gets a rating per 100 possessions multiplied by his projected minutes, minutes left by absent players are redistributed according to context, and any remainder is filled at replacement level, about −2.0 to −2.7 per 100 ([Dunks & Threes metric comparison](https://dunksandthrees.com/blog/metric-comparison); [LEBRON](https://www.bball-index.com/lebron-introduction/)). EPM predicts every player's minutes for every game, updates them as official statuses arrive, and beats season-to-date minutes by about 15% ([Dunks & Threes](https://dunksandthrees.com/about/epm)). FiveThirtyEight found that a mix of 65% player-based (RAPTOR) ratings and 35% Elo predicted best, with Elo's weight ranging from 0 to 55% depending on how closely the depth chart matched recent lineups ([538 methodology](https://web.archive.org/web/20240101081135/https://fivethirtyeight.com/methodology/how-our-nba-predictions-work/)). Availability matters mostly for stars. In the 2026 playoffs oddsmakers priced Nikola Jokić's absence at 5.5 points, most other stars at 2-3 and role players at a point or less ([theScore](https://www.thescore.com/news/3531319)), and Dunks & Threes' injury-aware player model beats an opponent-adjusted net-rating baseline by 0.9-1.5 points of accuracy and 0.3-0.4 points of margin error, live and in backtest ([Dunks & Threes](https://dunksandthrees.com/about/epm)), although no public study isolates the gain from availability alone. Nor does the literature offer a shortcut through a fancier learner: a deep-network claim of 72% accuracy comes with no market comparison ([arXiv 2512.08591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08591)), and studies in other sports find that model choice matters little while market information dominates ([arXiv 2408.08331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08331); [arXiv 2605.16066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16066)). For this system the gradient-boosted learner can stay, but its team-strength input has to change. The new input is the probability that each player plays, taken from the injury report at forecast time, multiplied by his rating as of the game date and by his projected minutes. A Questionable player should enter as a mixture of a scenario with him and one without him rather than as a fraction of a player, because minutes do not redistribute in proportion.

## No path yet turns tonight's injury report into a forecast

As of 26 October 2026 the production model cannot score a single 2026-27 game, whether played or unplayed. The dashboard serves only rows already in the engineered dataset, which ends on 12 April 2026, and returns 404 for anything else ([app.py L591-597](../app.py)), and no code builds features for an unplayed game ([DURUM.md L546-553](../DURUM.md)). Three inputs behind the production features, the v5 impact cache, the player box-score listings and the team-box cache, stop at 2025-26. The first two can only be rebuilt from a phone-database snapshot taken on 12 September 2026 by a loader that no longer exists, from a database that is offline, on a clone without the `.env` file needed to reach it ([csv_master.py L4-8](../prediction_engines/csv_master.py); [DURUM.md L416-431](../DURUM.md)). Even the 28 games already played cannot be turned into features. The shipped models also leave out the last 308 games of 2025-26, the season is hard-coded as 2025_2026 in the training script, app, simulator and calibrator ([retrain_production.py L71-81, L133-141](../prediction_engines/retrain_production.py)), and nothing monitors calibration or drift once games are played. The project's staleness study found monthly retraining unnecessary, but that script defaults to the old leaky dataset described below, so the finding needs a rerun on honest features ([staleness.json](../output/staleness.json)).

Worse than the missing path are the failure modes a naive extension would hit without raising any error. An unplayed game that slipped into the master frame would become a home loss, because `home_win = (point_diff > 0)` evaluates false on a missing score, and then a 0-0 game when the builder fills every gap with zero ([csv_master.py L225-227](../prediction_engines/csv_master.py); [build_dataset_db.py L211](../prediction_engines/build_dataset_db.py)). A game without a team sheet is scored as a team with nobody available ([pregame_roster.py L285, L376](../prediction_engines/pregame_roster.py)), and the team-box cache, keyed only by season name, would keep serving an in-progress season from its first snapshot ([csv_master.py L126-176](../prediction_engines/csv_master.py)). The box-score vocabulary has already changed under the roster rule: 2026-27 listings run 16-18 players per team and use "Inactive – G League – Two-Way" and "Inactive – Coach's Decision", neither of which the unavailability pattern matches, so 10 of 42 inactive tags in six sampled games would count as dressed ([pregame_roster.py L91-93](../prediction_engines/pregame_roster.py)). The old observed-roster dataset, with identical column names but leaky meaning, is still the default input of four experiment scripts ([staleness.py L48](../prediction_engines/staleness.py); [seed_ensemble.py L43](../prediction_engines/seed_ensemble.py)). Completeness checks only test whether a file exceeds 64 bytes, the V3-to-V2 column renaming changes one field's meaning without validation ([retrieve_2025_2026_data.py L47-127](../data_retriaval/retrieve_2025_2026_data.py)), and data the NBA publishes late is never fetched again ([value_engine.py L565-575](../prediction_engines/value_engine.py)).

The data sources themselves keep shifting. nba_api reached v1.12.0 on 24 October 2026 while the project runs 1.11.4 from February, and stats.nba.com has stopped serving most V2 box scores and the V1 and V2 play-by-play, which leaves the V3 endpoints as the main path while the defensive and hustle V2 box scores still work ([nba_api releases](https://github.com/swar/nba_api/releases); [known upstream issues](https://github.com/swar/nba_api/pull/715)). Access is fragile. The maintainers advise one request a second from a non-cloud address ([nba_api AI-agent guide](https://github.com/swar/nba_api/blob/master/docs/ai_agents.md)), the service went dark from 7 to 10 October ([issue #696](https://github.com/swar/nba_api/issues/696)), and the faster cdn.nba.com live feeds refused every scripted request from the project machine on 25 October, browser headers included ([cdn.nba.com](https://cdn.nba.com/static/json/liveData/boxscore/boxscore_0022600001.json)). Tracking-based box scores took 1.4 to 2.5 days to appear in opening week while hustle data arrived overnight ([stats.nba.com](https://stats.nba.com/stats/boxscoreplayertrackv3?GameID=0022600099)), so features built from them need a two-day tolerance and a re-poll. 2026-27 game IDs do not follow date order, so the order of games must come from game dates ([league game log](https://stats.nba.com/stats/leaguegamelog?Season=2026-27&SeasonType=Regular%20Season&PlayerOrTeam=T)). And v1.11.2 fixed box-score parsers that had returned their tables in the wrong order to code reading them by position, which makes any data fetched with the releases just before it worth checking ([nba_api releases](https://github.com/swar/nba_api/releases)).

The injury data the forecast needs is openly available. The official PDFs sit at a predictable CDN address, at 15-minute intervals since 22 December 2025 and hourly or three times a day before that ([NBA Official](https://official.nba.com/nba-injury-report-2026-27-season/); [NBAStatsProject](https://github.com/kyyamakaimotoyk/NBAStatsProject)). The MIT-licensed `nbainjuries` package parses them from 2021-22 on ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/nbainjuries/)), and projects that find old reports through the Internet Archive reach back to December 2018 ([nba-injury-database](https://github.com/mrees002/nba-injury-database)). The design rule is to keep every snapshot with its publication time and take the latest one before a fixed cutoff, such as an hour before tip-off ([test-m11 issue #64](https://github.com/PirateKingInc/test-m11/issues/64)), because public tables that keep only the report closest to tip-off cannot reconstruct such a cutoff ([NBAStatsProject](https://github.com/kyyamakaimotoyk/NBAStatsProject)). No pre-game snapshot exists before 2018-19, so injury-aware training should start there. The terms of use suit a private project and not a public one: NBA.com allows its statistics for "private, non-commercial purposes" with attribution but not "in connection with any gambling activity" or in a service that "features a database" ([NBA.com Terms](https://www.nba.com/termsofuse)), Disney's terms forbid automated extraction from ESPN ([Disney Terms](https://disneytermsofuse.com/english/)), and Sports Reference bars using its content to train machine-learning models ([TOS Tracker](https://tostracker.app/document/sports-reference)).

The repair is mostly plumbing, and its order matters. CSV-to-table loaders behind a layer that normalises V2 and V3 files, with a schema contract for each file type, turn the phone database into an optional speed-up. The value and impact engines then run incrementally on new game IDs, and the roster sweep's player state persists so that it can answer "as of today". The target game's sheet comes from the current roster minus injury-report Outs, never from a box score, unplayed rows carry a flag and stay out of training, and a parity test requires the as-of builder to reproduce the batch dataset exactly for games already played. Around that core belong several safeguards: comment classification by prefix with a whitelist that fails on unseen strings, content checks of player points against team points and the play-by-play final, and a re-fetch of the last two or three days on every run. Caches should be keyed by their set of game IDs, builds should stop on failure instead of filling zeros, and dependencies should be pinned, since [requirements.txt](../requirements.txt) omits flask, waitress, scipy, xgboost and pymysql and sets only lower bounds on the rest. Last, every forecast should be logged with a timestamp before tip-off, which is also what turns 2026-27 into the untouched test set the evaluation lacks.

## The simulator and value engine rebuild what public ratings already provide

### The simulator's errors come from minutes, not game mechanics

The possession-level season simulator is well engineered — sandboxed, reproducible down to the score, calibrated to measured league proportions, and deliberately independent of the trained models so that it can test them — but as a forecaster it only matches the naive baseline. Over five backtested seasons its win projections correlated 0.53 with reality, with a mean error of 8.7 wins, against 0.58 and 8.8 for "same as last season" ([DURUM.md L205-211](../DURUM.md)). On the first 28 games of 2026-27, replayed 1,000 times, it picked 18 winners but scored a Brier of 0.225 and a log loss of 0.642, behind last season's records converted to log5 probabilities at 0.218 and 0.622 ([DURUM.md L449-465](../DURUM.md)). Twenty-eight games prove little either way, but the player data show where the errors come from. Simulated minutes correlate only 0.67 with real ones, rookies with no NBA history get replacement-level roles (Boozer played 70.5 real minutes over two games against 0.6 simulated, Acuff 71.5 against 4.7), and Ingram logged 64.8 simulated minutes while inactive ([sim_vs_real_2026_2027.json](../output/sim_vs_real_2026_2027.json)).

The structural causes are visible in [season_sim.py](../prediction_engines/season_sim.py). Minutes come frozen from each player's last season, with the top ten scaled to 240 (L356-364, L467-480), and availability is last season's share of games played, driven by a two-state Markov chain that ignores known absences (L541-558). Three team effects travel with players at full strength (L138, L1016). A score-effect setting tuned to match 2025-26's margin spread compresses the differences between teams to 80% of reality and the overtime rate to 2-3% against 4.4% ([DURUM.md L193-198](../DURUM.md)). Every team also draws the same seasonal strength shock, which widens 10-90% win ranges to about 30 wins and flattens single games: Detroit against Boston came out at 0.502, against 0.612 from last season's records ([sim_vs_real_2026_2027.json](../output/sim_vs_real_2026_2027.json)). The mechanics were tuned and validated on the same 2025-26 season, and nothing updates as results arrive. The league environment, as DURUM already concludes, is not the problem the opening week suggested. Fouls ran above the season average in eight of the nine opening weeks since 2017-18, free throws and fouls ran 13-15% above the full-season rate in each of the last two Octobers, and 2026-27's opening week is statistically indistinguishable from 2025-26's on both ([stats.nba.com opening week](https://stats.nba.com/stats/leaguedashteamstats?Season=2025-26&SeasonType=Regular%20Season&PerMode=Totals&MeasureType=Base&DateFrom=10/21/2025&DateTo=10/27/2025); [stats.nba.com by month](https://stats.nba.com/stats/leaguedashteamstats?Season=2025-26&SeasonType=Regular%20Season&PerMode=Totals&MeasureType=Base&Month=1)). The simulator therefore needs month-of-season multipliers rather than refitted constants. No playing rule changed for 2026-27, and the league issued only points of emphasis on push-offs, two-handed screens and flopping ([NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-referees-teams-points-of-emphasis-2026-27)).

Published simulators differ from this one mainly in their inputs and in updating. FiveThirtyEight rated every game on a team's schedule from the injury and transaction list, kept injured players off the depth chart until their estimated return, ran 50,000 seasons "hot" so that ratings moved after each simulated result, and blended minutes as (12.6 × preseason minutes per game + current-season minutes) / (12.6 + games played) ([538 methodology](https://web.archive.org/web/20240101081135/https://fivethirtyeight.com/methodology/how-our-nba-predictions-work/)). EPM runs 30,000 nightly simulations with projected lineups and simulated availability ([Dunks & Threes](https://dunksandthrees.com/about/epm)), and DARKO rates a rookie before his debut from age, draft slot and height, then lets his game data take over quickly ([DARKO](https://darko.app/about)). The benchmark matters as much as the method. In the APBR preseason win-projection contest the BetMGM and ESPN BET win totals finished first and second in 2025-26, with mean errors of 7.39 and 7.40 wins, ahead of DARKO at 8.48, while in 2024-25 the market finished eighth of 17 at 8.40 behind a winner at 7.50 ([APBR 2025-26](https://apbr.org/metrics/viewtopic.php?p=44892); [APBR 2024-25](https://apbr.org/metrics/viewtopic.php?t=10146&start=240)). The simulator should be judged against market win totals, which several published forecasters also use as priors ([Neil Paine](https://neilpaine.substack.com/p/2025-26-nba-elo-forecast-and-player); [TeamRankings](https://betiq.teamrankings.com/articles/preseason-rankings-ratings-explain/)). It should also expect late-season patterns learned from past seasons to shift, because the 2026-27 lottery reform demotes the three worst records and gives the league new powers against tanking ([NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-board-governors-approve-new-draft-lottery-system)).

### The value engine counts box-score events; public metrics estimate impact

Pricing 5.1 of the value engine fixed real bugs, charging misses, getting rebound types right and finding the passer for 99.8-100% of assists, and it raised the score's correlation with on-court plus-minus from 0.14 to about 0.42 ([DURUM.md L132-164, L252-303](../DURUM.md)). It is still box-score accounting. Defence beyond steals (+1.15), blocks (+0.6) and defensive rebounds (+0.26) is invisible. Credits do not add up to points, since an assist pays 30% of the basket's value on top of the scorer's full credit and a steal and its turnover are both booked at 1.15. Pricing against 1.00 points per possession, below the league's roughly 1.08, makes ordinary-efficiency shot volume worth something, which is why Russell Westbrook's 2017-18 season still rates +7.9 per game ([value_engine.py L25-55, L153](../prediction_engines/value_engine.py); [DURUM.md L300-303](../DURUM.md)). There is no adjustment for teammates or opponents and no shrinkage, so a weekly rating can rest on one game ([power_rankings.py L318-323](../prediction_engines/power_rankings.py)), and the validation target, same-season raw plus-minus, is dominated by teammates and shares the player's own points. The headline claim that team value totals correlate 0.95 with margins (0.80 for v4) is largely an accounting identity, and switching the model's inputs from v4 to v5 left every forecast metric statistically indistinguishable, as DURUM itself records ([impact_v5_summary.json](../output/impact_v5_summary.json); [DURUM.md L305-308](../DURUM.md)).

For forecasting, importing an established as-of rating beats refining a box-score count. The public options differ in method, history and terms.

| Metric | Method and evidence | History, access and terms |
|---|---|---|
| EPM | RAPM with a box-score-and-tracking Bayesian prior; best retrodiction error in its author's 2020 comparison, RMSE 2.48 against 2.63 for RAPTOR, 2.71 for BPM, 2.80 for plain RAPM and 3.20 for PER ([Dunks & Threes metric comparison](https://dunksandthrees.com/blog/metric-comparison)) | Daily history since 2001-02 behind an annual subscription whose terms allow personal, non-commercial use and forbid scraping ([Dunks & Threes terms](https://dunksandthrees.com/terms)) |
| DARKO (DPM) | Box-score projections with a plus-minus layer, updated after every game | Free, with CSV downloads and a weekly "Time Machine" back to 1996-97; no published terms, so automated pulls need the owner's consent ([DARKO](https://darko.app/)) |
| LEBRON | RAPM with a box-score prior | From 2009-10 ([BBall Index](https://www.bball-index.com/lebron-introduction/)) |
| BPM | Box-score estimate of plus-minus | Free, but Sports Reference's terms bar machine-learning use |
| RAPTOR | FiveThirtyEight's player metric | Frozen since 2023, under a CC BY 4.0 licence ([fivethirtyeight/data](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data)) |
| Net Points | ESPN metric introduced in March 2025 | Explicitly descriptive, not predictive ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/44093220/introducing-best-nba-metric-yet-net-points-amazing-early-findings)) |

The same 2020 test showed that rookie handling matters: valuing rookies at replacement level gave an error of 2.48, against 2.62 at league average. DARKO is the natural first import, since it is free, updated after every game and available as of any week since 1996-97, which is the as-of history that leak-free training needs; EPM is the better-documented option if a subscription is acceptable. The in-house engine can then keep doing what it does well, a transparent descriptive rating for the dashboard, once it prices against league-average efficiency per 100 possessions, splits credit within a possession so that totals add up to points, shrinks small samples, and is validated on whether early-season values predict later team net rating.

## Betting lines improve the forecast only by replacing it

### What the market lab measured

The project assembled opening and closing lines for 10,777 regular-season games, nine full seasons plus the first 28 games of 2026-27, from the Sportsbook Reviews archive through 16 January 2023 and from ESPN's odds service after that. Every score matches the project's own, and closing spreads differ by 0.10 points on the 118 games both sources cover ([DURUM.md L485-516](../DURUM.md); [odds_history.csv](../odds_data/odds_history.csv)). [market_lab.py](../prediction_engines/market_lab.py) then compared four forecasts, paired within the same 54 month-seed cells of the production walk-forward: the production model; the same model with the closing line's de-vigged log-odds, spread and total added as tree features; a stack, meaning a two-weight logistic regression on the classifier's and the market's log-odds fitted on the last 10% of each training window; and the market alone.

| Arm (3,224 games, 54 cells) | Accuracy | Brier | Log loss | Margin MAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model: classifier (shipped blend) | 67.0% (68.9%) | 0.212 (0.204) | 0.613 (0.595) | 11.23 |
| Model + odds as tree features: classifier (blend) | 64.8% (70.2%) | 0.219 (0.199) | 0.627 (0.583) | 10.87 |
| Stack of classifier and market | 70.1% | 0.194 | 0.570 | — |
| Market alone | 70.2% | 0.194 | 0.569 | 10.72 |

Source: [market_lab.json](../output/market_lab.json); the blend log losses are my averages of the per-cell values.

The odds lift the forecast by 3 points of accuracy and 0.018 Brier over the classifier, but the stack simply is the market, trailing the line alone by 0.0004 Brier (month-clustered SE 0.0004) and 0.1 points of accuracy (SE 0.3) (my calculation from the cells). The weights explain why. The market's log-odds get a coefficient of 1.11 and the model's −0.29, so the stack keeps the line and leans slightly against wherever the model departs from it, and when the classifier and the line picked different winners the line was right 60% of the time. The −0.29 itself is imprecise, because the stack is fitted without regularisation (C = 10⁶) on 700-1,000 calibration games with two strongly correlated inputs: cell weights swing from −3.2 to +0.6 and monthly means from −2.1 to +0.5, a wider range than the −0.9 to +0.5 that DURUM reports. Still, no cell found information the market lacked. Two details matter for the follow-up DURUM plans: both the stack and the disagreement count use the classifier rather than the shipped blend, and the 1,505 "games" of disagreement are game-seed instances summed over three seeds, about 500 games per seed ([market_lab.py L100-120](../prediction_engines/market_lab.py)). Feeding the line to the trees made the classifier worse, by 2.2 points of accuracy and 0.0067 Brier, with early stopping cutting the tree count from 127 to 47, because a boosted tree ensemble can only approximate a smooth log-odds input with coarse steps. The blend recovered only because its logistic and margin components absorbed the line, and it still finished 0.0049 Brier behind the market alone. The 308-game encompassing regression had already shown the same thing, with a market coefficient of 1.36 and a model coefficient of −0.07 whose interval runs from −1.06 to 0.82 ([audit check B](../research_notes/NBA%20prediction%20system%20audit%202026/checks/prod_check2.py)).

### The narrowing gap is a lead, not yet a finding

Split by season, the cells show the model closing in on the market (my calculation from [market_lab.json](../output/market_lab.json)).

| Season | Blend's Brier gap to the market | Market right when the two disagreed | Stack's average model weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023-24 | 0.0149 | 65.2% | −0.83 |
| 2024-25 | 0.0112 | 61.4% | −0.35 |
| 2025-26 | 0.0053 | 49.9% | +0.30 |

Three caveats keep this from being a finding. The yardstick weakened, because the 2023-24 closing line is the median of about eleven books while 2024-25 and 2025-26 use a single book each, ESPN BET and then DraftKings from 1 December 2025 ([odds_history.csv](../odds_data/odds_history.csv); [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/media/espn-bet-shutting-down-enters-new-gambling-agreement-draftkings)). The recipe and pricing were selected on these same folds. And even in 2025-26 the stack did not beat the line out of sample (+0.0009 Brier). A prospective 2026-27 comparison against a consensus line is the clean test of whether the model has begun to carry information of its own.

DURUM supposes that the disagreements reflect injury news the model does not see, but in the backtest they do not. The roster block already knows the final active list, so the model differs from the line in how it values absences, minutes, rest and newcomers, which are exactly the inputs the earlier sections found missing. In live use the model would lose even that hindsight, so at a real forecast time the gap would be wider than 0.010 Brier, not narrower. The same hindsight is a trap for the opening-line test DURUM lists next. Opening probabilities exist in the file only from 2023-24, exactly the walk-forward's window, and they are informative because news moves them: in 2025-26 the home team covered the opening spread 62.1% of the time when the line then moved 1.5 points or more toward it, and 33.3% of the time when it moved away ([Kaggle notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/minmaxer/nba-2025-26-closing-lines-favourites-and-ats)). A model whose roster features encode the final inactive list will "predict" those moves and appear to beat the opening line by knowing the future. The test means something only if availability is taken from the injury report as it stood when the line opened, which the archive described above can supply for 2023-24 through 2025-26.

### Where odds earn their keep

Is cross-referencing odds worth it? As an input to the forecast the project ships, no. That experiment has been run, a "model + market" number is the market reached by a longer route, and odds as tree features make the model worse. As information, yes, in four roles that are cheap because the data and test harness already exist. The first role is the gate: a feature or recipe earns its place only if it lifts the stack above the market alone on paired, month-clustered Brier and log loss, a sharper test than the model's own accuracy, and the stack should be run on the shipped blend rather than the classifier. The second is the opening-line test done properly, with availability as reported when the line opened, alongside a check that needs no betting at all: whether the model's departure from the opening line predicts which way the line moves by the close. If the model knows anything the market does not, it will show a day ahead, before the news is priced, and a residual design measures it best, either LightGBM boosting from the market's log-odds as its starting score or a regularised stack. The third is market priors where they are hardest to beat, using preseason win totals for team strength in the first weeks and as both prior and benchmark for the simulator. The fourth is the disagreement games, de-duplicated across seeds and mined as a labelled set for the availability work. The lines should stay offline throughout. The ESPN portion of the archive, everything from January 2023 on, was collected at one request per second although Disney's terms forbid automated extraction, so a licensed source is the defensible route for continued collection; The Odds API offers NBA history from June 2020, with five-minute snapshots since September 2022, on paid plans starting at $30 a month ([The Odds API](https://the-odds-api.com/historical-odds-data/); [The Odds API pricing](https://the-odds-api.com/)). NBA.com's statistics terms rule out pairing NBA data with betting content in anything public ([NBA.com Terms](https://www.nba.com/termsofuse)). The project's rule that odds are a yardstick and never advice fits all four roles.

## Plumbing and evidence come before any new model

The fixes depend on one another, and those dependencies set their order.

| Step | Build | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | As-of feature pipeline: CSV loaders with schema contracts, incremental value and impact engines, persisted roster state, a parity test, and hard failures on missing labels, empty team sheets, unseen comment strings and stale caches | Any 2026-27 forecast at all |
| 2 | Injury-report archive: every 15-minute snapshot from today, backfilled to 2021-22 with `nbainjuries` and toward 2018-19 through the Internet Archive; statuses calibrated into play probabilities by team and injury type | Honest availability features and a valid opening-line test |
| 3 | Prospective forecast log: frozen recipe, a fixed lead time such as an hour before tip-off, timestamps, weekly scoring against the market | An untouched test set and the first honest early-season numbers |
| 4 | Evaluation repair: an archive of per-game walk-forward predictions from each component model, Brier and log loss as primary metrics, month-block bootstrap, correction for multiple comparisons, a separate early-season group, labs that call the production fit path, and the market stack as the acceptance gate | The power to see real gains of a few thousandths of Brier and to reject false ones |
| 5 | Team strength from report-time availability × as-of player ratings (DARKO or EPM) × projected minutes, with rookie priors and preseason priors (last season regressed to the mean, roster continuity, market win totals) | The information gap the market exploits |
| 6 | Schedule and opponent features: rest, back-to-backs, travel, time zones, altitude, a home edge that varies over time, per-100 ratings and the four factors, NaN instead of zero, all re-screened on paired Brier | Documented effects of one to two points per game |
| 7 | A fitted, calibrated blend: weights from logistic stacking in a nested walk-forward, Platt or beta recalibration, score intervals centred on the shipped predictions; then the opening-line test | Sharper probabilities from the same signal and an answer to the day-ahead question |
| 8 | Simulator and dashboard: a minutes model with rookie priors, the injury list, in-season updating and month multipliers, judged against market win totals; an honest header, an early-season warning, badges based on the value score, and standings ordered by the replication mean | User-facing numbers that match what was measured |

## Conclusion

The market lab changes what success should mean here. At tip-off the best available probability is already public, and nothing in this audit suggests a box-score model will out-forecast it on average. The model's value lies in what the closing line cannot do: forecast a day ahead, explain why a line sits where it does, and drive a season simulator. Each of those can be measured without betting, by whether the model's departures from the opening line anticipate the close, whether its stack weight turns positive on honest features, and whether the simulator beats market win totals. All three need the same missing piece, a timestamped record from the injury report of who is expected to play, which makes the injury archive and the as-of pipeline, not a new learner, the highest-leverage work in the repository.

The most intriguing number in the audit is also the least settled. In 2025-26 the model's Brier gap to the closing line fell to 0.005, and the market won only half their disagreements. That is either a model beginning to carry information of its own or a yardstick that weakened when the number of books fell from eleven to one, and 2026-27 will decide which, but only if the system can forecast those games before they are played, using availability it could actually have known at the time. The habit that caught two leaks and keeps every negative result, measuring before believing, is exactly what that test needs.
